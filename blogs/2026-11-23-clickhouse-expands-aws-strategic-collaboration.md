# ClickHouse expands AWS strategic collaboration with a $1.5 billion-plus agreement to scale its unified enterprise AI data stack

_Five-year agreement establishes a framework for ClickHouse investing more than $1.5 billion in potential software, services, and solutions on AWS, with increased investment in customer migrations, integrations, and go-to-market programs._

**SAN FRANCISCO — November 23, 2026** — ClickHouse, a leading AI data platform, today announced an expanded five-year strategic collaboration agreement (SCA) with Amazon Web Services (AWS) to help enterprises build, observe, and secure AI applications on AWS. The agreement establishes a framework for ClickHouse investing more than $1.5 billion to scale its unified enterprise AI data stack on AWS, supporting customer migrations, technical integrations for agents built on AWS, and initiatives to drive enterprise adoption.

ClickHouse Cloud is built on AWS, launched in 2022 and now serves more than 5,000 customers worldwide. ClickHouse has quadrupled its customer count on AWS since signing its first SCA with AWS in December 2024, and now holds [seven AWS Competencies](/partners/aws).

“AWS was the foundation for ClickHouse Cloud from the day we launched it, and the December 2024 agreement gave us the structure to expand our collaboration,” said Aaron Katz, CEO of ClickHouse. “Since then, our growth on AWS has outpaced our estimates. Our customers are expanding from real-time analytics into transactions, data warehousing, observability, security, and AI engineering, and they want those workloads on an open, composable foundation so their developers and AI agents can build, observe, and secure applications in one place. This expanded agreement will help customers migrate workloads and put real-time analytics and AI into production, from detecting fraud to improving streaming experiences, helping them run applications efficiently as demand grows.”

“Enterprises are moving AI agents from pilots into production, and those agents need fast access to live business data,” said Matt Yanchyshyn, VP, AWS Marketplace and Partner Services. “With this expanded agreement, ClickHouse is investing in its unified enterprise AI data stack on AWS. That gives customers more ways to modernize their data infrastructure, connect ClickHouse data to the agents they build on AWS, and put those agents to work across their business.”

## What the expanded agreement includes
The expanded agreement allows for go-to-market funding from AWS of up to four times the level under the December 2024 agreement, supporting proof-of-concept and migration programs that move analytics, data warehousing, observability, and security workloads from legacy systems to ClickHouse Cloud on AWS. ClickHouse will deepen ClickHouse Cloud integrations with Amazon Bedrock, Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, and Amazon Quick, so AI agents built on AWS can query live ClickHouse data through the Model Context Protocol (MCP). ClickHouse will also extend that work to new AWS services as they launch. On AWS, ClickHouse will focus its engineering capabilities on agentic workloads, including agent observability and evals with Langfuse, transactional state and memory for agents in ClickHouse Managed Postgres, and AI-assisted security operations in ClickHouse Security. ClickHouse Cloud on AWS will continue to run on AWS Graviton by default and expand to additional AWS regions and deployment options.

## An expanded platform for enterprise AI
On November 9, ClickHouse [announced](/blog/announcing-clickhouse-platform-largest-expansion) a major platform expansion that unifies traditionally siloed systems to support agentic-ready enterprise data infrastructure. ClickHouse Managed Postgres brings transactional data onto the platform; ClickHouse Observability brings logs and traces alongside it; and ClickHouse Security adds security telemetry. Langfuse, the open-source AI engineering platform ClickHouse acquired in January, adds an AI Gateway for visibility and control over model usage, alongside enterprise-ready tracing, evaluation, and monitoring for AI applications and agents. All of it runs on the same open, composable foundation as ClickHouse’s analytics engine, so developers and the agents they deploy can build, observe, and secure data-intensive applications in one place.

ClickHouse Managed Postgres is generally available in ClickHouse Cloud on AWS, ClickHouse Observability is in public beta, and ClickHouse Security is in private preview. On November 9, ClickHouse also announced its acquisition of Xata, the branch-native Postgres company, which will bring instant database branching and scale-to-zero environments to Managed Postgres.

ClickHouse’s remote MCP server connects to [Amazon Quick](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/apn/how-clickhouse-powers-real-time-ai-agents-on-amazon-quick/) so users can query live ClickHouse data in natural language. Langfuse integrates with Amazon Bedrock, and [ClickHouse Cloud is available in AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-p4gwofrqpkltu).

Customers are already expanding how they use ClickHouse Cloud on AWS. “At Sony LIV, faster visibility translates directly into a better viewer experience and improved platform reliability. ClickHouse has become a foundational layer in our real-time analytics and observability modernization journey,” said Vaibhav Gupta, VP of Platform Engineering at Sony LIV. “With ClickHouse’s expanded collaboration with AWS, we are looking forward to further innovation leveraging AWS services and integrated support for our next generation of large-scale OTT streaming operations.”
